The history of Art

Art has evolved from early cave drawings focused on survival, to religious and symbolic works in ancient and medieval times, then to realistic and human-centered art during the Renaissance, followed by creative experimentation in modern art, and finally to today's diverse and innovative contemporary art using new ideas and technology.

Early artworks were not just decorative but served important purposes like story telling and teaching hunting skills. As humans settles into civilizations such as those in Egypt Mesopotamia and Greeece, art evolved into more structured forms, including sculptures, architecture and detailed paintings that often focused on daily life and mythology.
During the Renaissance, artists began to study science, anatomy, and perspective, creating more realistic and lifeworks, as seen in famous pieces like the The mona lisa.

Over time, movements such as Impressionism and Mordernism encouraged artists to experiment with colour, light, abstract, ideas rather than strict realism. Today art has expanded into many forms, including digital design, photography, animation and many more other forms.

The Prehistoric cave paintings

Cave paintings depicting how early humans lived and expressed themselves during the Old Stone age period. These figures have been created on natural rock surface, showing uneven textures, cracks and earthy colours.

The materials that were present in this era we charcoal and mineral pigments to create both outlines and filled shape of the rock to give their drawings a sense of depth and movement. These artworks provide valuable insight into the environment, and thinking of early human societies.
